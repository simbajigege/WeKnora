SUMMARY: Malaysia's 2026 NIF manufacturing guidelines define eligible applicants, tax incentive options, NIA scorecard evaluation, tiering mechanics, compliance duties, exclusions, and sustainability requirements.

# MIDA NIF Manufacturing Guidelines 2026

The document explains Malaysia's New Incentive Framework for new manufacturing investments. It describes [[entity/new-incentive-framework|NIF]] as a phased, tiered, and outcome-based framework introduced through National Budget 2026 and aligned with [[entity/national-investment-aspirations|NIA]] and [[entity/new-industrial-master-plan-2030|NIMP 2030]].

## Incentive Options

Applicants must choose one mutually exclusive tax incentive for a qualifying project:

- [[concept/special-tax-rate|Special Tax Rate]]: a reduced corporate income tax rate on taxable income for a specified period.
- [[concept/investment-tax-allowance|Investment Tax Allowance]]: an allowance based on qualifying capital expenditure that can offset statutory income.

The incentive ranges vary by category. New investments may receive an STR between 0% and 10% for up to 15 years, or ITA of up to 100% for up to 15 years. Less Developed Area projects may receive an STR between 0% and 15% for up to 15 years. Small companies may receive an STR between 3% and 12% for up to 15 years.

## Eligible Applicants

Eligible applicants are companies incorporated under the Companies Act 2016 and resident in Malaysia. The guidelines cover new or existing companies undertaking new manufacturing investments. Fifteen manufacturing subsectors are listed, including E&E, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, aerospace, machinery and equipment, automotive, petroleum products and petrochemicals, oleochemicals, food production and processing, wood, textile, strategic minerals-based products, rubber-based products, and metal.

Companies producing biotechnology-based or recycled products may apply under the relevant manufacturing subsectors. Companies must also keep separate accounts for activities approved with tax incentives and those without tax incentives.

## Application Flow

The application process moves through consultation, online submission, [[concept/nia-scorecard|NIA Scorecard]] evaluation, principle approval, and compliance monitoring. Applications are submitted via the InvestMalaysia platform. Incomplete applications are returned.

[[entity/malaysian-investment-development-authority|MIDA]] issues the principle approval letter after approval. The letter specifies the approved incentive, tiering level, and conditions that must be met during the incentive period.

## Scorecard And Tiering

The [[concept/nia-scorecard|NIA Scorecard]] evaluates project quality and contribution to national objectives. The scorecard covers pillars such as economic complexity, high-valued job opportunities, domestic linkages, industrial cluster development, inclusivity, and sustainability practices.

The [[concept/tiering-approach|tiering approach]] makes the incentive conditional on the company's proposed commitments. Better scorecard outcomes correspond to better incentive packages. Minimum-condition compliance gives Tier 2 treatment; compliance with both minimum and additional conditions gives Tier 1 treatment.

## Compliance Mechanics

For [[concept/special-tax-rate|Special Tax Rate]], companies must apply before commencing operations, then apply for determination of the commencement year of assessment within 24 months from the principle approval letter. They must submit an Annual Compliance Report to [[entity/malaysian-investment-development-authority|MIDA]] within seven months after the end of each year of assessment.

For [[concept/investment-tax-allowance|Investment Tax Allowance]], companies must apply before commencing operations and submit commencement-date determination plus minimum-condition compliance declaration within 36 months from the principle approval letter. Compliance declarations verified by external auditors are due within seven months after every five-year ITA period.

## Effective Date And International Tax Context

Applications received by [[entity/malaysian-investment-development-authority|MIDA]] starting from 1 March 2026 fall under these guidelines. The document also notes Malaysia's [[concept/global-minimum-tax|Global Minimum Tax]] implementation from 2025 under Pillar Two, including possible top-up tax for groups with annual global revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

## Key Takeaways

- NIF moves Malaysia's manufacturing incentives toward measurable outcomes and tiered benefits.
- Applicants choose either STR or ITA; the selection is final once accepted by MIDA.
- Eligibility depends on company status, manufacturing subsector, sector-specific criteria, and exclusions.
- The NIA Scorecard is the core assessment mechanism for project quality.
- Compliance reporting determines whether approved companies enjoy Tier 1, Tier 2, or no incentive for the relevant period.
